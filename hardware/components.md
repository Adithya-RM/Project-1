# Hardware Components & Signal Acquisition Protocol

This document details the physical sensor selection, anatomical placement strategy, hardware signal conditioning circuit design, and standardized acquisition protocol used for Vibroarthrographic (VAG) knee signal capture.

---

## 1. Piezoelectric Patch Sensor

### Specification & Properties
- **Sensor Type**: Flexible Piezoelectric Polymer Patch Sensor.
- **Operating Principle**: Converts mechanical strain and surface acoustic vibrations generated by patellofemoral joint movement into proportional electric voltage signals ($V \propto \Delta x$).
- **Frequency Response**: $0.1 \text{ Hz} - 1 \text{ kHz}$ broadband sensitivity.

### Why Piezoelectric Sensing for VAG?
1. **High Transient Sensitivity**: Piezoelectric transducers exhibit extreme sensitivity to impulsive acoustic emissions and transient micro-vibrations resulting from cartilage-on-cartilage or meniscal surface friction.
2. **Minimal Mass Loading**: The lightweight patch sensor adds negligible mechanical mass to the patella, preventing motion damping or artificial artifact generation.
3. **High Dynamic Range**: Capable of resolving both low-amplitude smooth joint glides and high-energy pathological friction bursts.

---

## 2. Sensor Placement & Anatomical Rationale

### Placement Site: Central Patella
The piezoelectric patch sensor is positioned centrally over the **patella** and secured using medical-grade double-sided adhesive tape. Position consistency across trials and subjects is verified by skin-safe anatomical marking.

![Sensor Setup](../hardware/sensor_setup.jpg)

### Why Patella Placement?
During preliminary comparative trials evaluating the patella, lateral condyle, and medial condyle:
- **Mechanical Fulcrum**: The patella functions as a sesamoid bone and mechanical fulcrum redirecting the force vector of the quadriceps tendon during knee extension.
- **Vibration Concentration**: Surface acoustic emissions generated at patellofemoral and tibiofemoral articular interfaces converge on the patella.
- **Highest Amplitude**: Empirical measurements confirmed that patellar placement consistently yields **maximum signal-to-noise ratio (SNR)** and distinct acoustic burst separation compared to condylar anatomical landmarks.

## 3. Two-Resistor Signal Conditioning Circuit

To maintain hardware simplicity, cost-effectiveness, and ease of replication without requiring active operational amplifiers or external power supplies, a two-resistor passive conditioning circuit was engineered.

![Circuit Diagram](../hardware/circuit_diagram.png)

### Circuit Topology
```
Piezo Sensor (+) ────┬───────[ 100 kΩ ]───────┐
                     │                         │
                     ├───────[  1 kΩ  ]───────┴──── Output (+) to Measurement Device
                     │                         │
Piezo Sensor (-) ────┴─────────────────────────┴──── Output (-) Ground
```

### Components & Rationale
1. **100 kΩ Bias & Load Resistor**:
   - The piezoelectric element acts as a high-impedance charge source ($Z_{in} > 1 \text{ M}\Omega$).
   - The $100 \text{ k}\Omega$ resistor provides an essential high-impedance load matching and DC bias discharge path, preventing sensor saturation and charge accumulation.
2. **1 kΩ Attenuation Resistor**:
   - Forms the lower leg of the passive voltage divider.
   - Provides a fixed attenuation scaling factor ($\alpha = \frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2} = \frac{1\text{ k}\Omega}{100\text{ k}\Omega + 1\text{ k}\Omega} \approx 0.0099$), scaling high transient voltage spikes into a safe $\pm 1\text{ V}$ input range suitable for direct ADC acquisition.

## 4. Measurement Device & Data Acquisition Protocol

### Setup Hardware
- **Measurement Interface**: Digital Oscilloscope / USB Data Acquisition (DAQ) module.
- **Sampling Frequency**: $f_s = 1000 \text{ Hz}$ (Nyquist limit = $500 \text{ Hz}$).
- **Resolution**: 12-bit ADC with real-time CSV export.

![Oscilloscope Setup](../hardware/oscilloscope_setup.jpg)

### Standardized Acquisition Protocol
1. **Subject Positioning**: Subjects are seated on an elevated platform with knees flexed at $90^\circ$ and lower legs hanging freely without ground contact.
2. **Skin Preparation**: Patellar skin surface cleaned with 70% isopropyl alcohol to minimize skin-sensor contact impedance.
3. **Paced Flexion-Extension**: Subjects perform full active range-of-motion knee extension ($90^\circ \rightarrow 0^\circ \rightarrow 90^\circ$) governed by an audible metronome at **0.5 Hz** (2 seconds per extension/flexion cycle).
4. **Recording Duration**: 5 to 10 seconds continuous signal acquisition stored as time-stamped CSV arrays (`time`, `voltage`).
